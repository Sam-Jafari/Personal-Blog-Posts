# LinkedIn share post (feed teaser, posts 1 to 3 days after Medium)

My YouTube feed went bad a few weeks ago and it took me a while to work out why.

YouTube is my television. It knows me well: what I click and what I watch to the end. It has never once asked why. So one click on a political argument I found absurd became a week of political commentary, and one curious look at an aircraft accident became "likes aviation disasters."

That gap between what grabs our attention and what we actually want more of is the whole problem with recommendation systems, and the research on it is not subtle. One extra negative word in a headline lifts clicks. One extra reference to the political out-group lifts shares by about two thirds. Nobody has to design for outrage. The objective finds it on its own.

I wrote up what I think the fix looks like. Two parts. The platform shows you the person it thinks you are, the way Facebook once showed me a buried page of everything it had inferred about me. Then you correct it in plain language, which generative AI finally makes possible. YouTube has started down this road with its custom feed prompt. Right now it is a chip on the side of the product. It should be the product.
